Is South Korea Safe for Plastic Surgery?

South Korea is a go-to destination for plastic surgery, but one important question remains: Is it safe to get plastic surgery in South Korea?

When we talk about safety, we mean having a qualified and licensed surgeon, a well-equipped and properly regulated clinic, and reliable support after the procedure.

In this blog, we’ll take a closer look at the safety of South Korea’s plastic surgery industry. We’ll explore how the system is regulated, what qualifications surgeons must have, and the warning signs to watch out for when choosing a clinic.

Regulation and Oversight of Plastic Surgery in South Korea

When it comes to plastic surgery, safety is a top priority and South Korea takes it seriously. The country has a well-regulated system to make sure both local and international patients receive high-quality and safe care.

Two major organizations are responsible for overseeing the plastic surgery field:

  • The Korean Society of Plastic and Reconstructive Surgeons (KSPRS)
  • The Korean Medical Association (KMA)

These groups help set ethical standards, provide training to surgeons, and promote safe practices across the industry.

For international patients, the rules are even stricter. Clinics that treat foreign visitors must be registered with the Ministry of Health and Welfare. Many also choose to get certified through the Korean Accreditation Program for Hospitals Serving Foreign Patients (KAHF). This program checks over 120 safety points, including:

  • Malpractice insurance
  • Emergency systems
  • Infection control procedures

To improve trust and transparency, South Korea also requires cameras in operating rooms and public lists of the medical staff involved in surgeries ( HMS Review ).

Doctors who want to perform plastic surgery legally in South Korea must follow a long and strict process:

  • Complete six years of medical school
  • Pass national licensing exams
  • Receive additional specialized training in plastic and reconstructive surgery to become board-certified

How to Choose a Safe Clinic or Surgeon?

Choosing a safe clinic or surgeon in South Korea requires careful research and attention to detail. Here are some practical tips to help you make an informed decision:

Verify certifications

Make sure the surgeon is certified by the Korean Society of Plastic and Reconstructive Surgeons (KSPRS).

You can do this by searching for the doctor on the KSPRS website. Moreover, check if the surgeon has membership in respected international organizations like the International Society of Aesthetic Plastic Surgery (ISAPS).

Review before-and-after photos

Look for results that match your ethnicity and the specific procedure you want. This gives you a realistic idea of the surgeon’s skill and style.

Ask for patient references or independent reviews

Hearing from past patients, especially other international clients, can give an idea about the clinic’s service, outcomes, and aftercare.

Check platforms like Sungyesa (a Korean review forum) or Google reviews for patient experiences. But always cross-check for authenticity and look for consistent feedback across multiple sources.

Check for clear communication and personalized care

The clinic should provide a proper consultation, answer all your questions, and offer support in your language or translation services.

When planning a medical trip to South Korea, it's important to consider not only the cost of the procedure but also the associated expenses. Plastic surgery prices in this country remain competitive as of 2025—30-50% lower than in the United States. FAQ

What is rhinoplasty surgery?

Rhinoplasty is a surgical procedure which can alter the size, shape and profile of your nose. It is commonly referred to as a ‘nose job.’

During surgery, bumps / humps can be removed; a deviated nose can be straightened; a broad nose can be narrowed; and nostrils can be resized or reshaped. The tip of the nose can also be refined and reangled.

The procedure involves removing any excess bone and / or cartilage and reshaping the remainder.

The key types of rhinoplasty include:

Closed Rhinoplasty: This is the most common type of rhinoplasty, as it is less invasive, yet can deliver significant results.

All the incisions are hidden inside the nose and the surgery is performed inside the nostrils, preventing any visible scarring.

This surgery is generally completed faster than open rhinoplasty and there is less nasal tissue irritation, which means healing and recovery time is usually shorter.

Open Rhinoplasty: Also known as external rhinoplasty, this type of surgery is more invasive and is often used for patients who require more extensive work on their nose.

This technique is usually suited to patients who have structural nasal issues or who have suffered severe trauma to the nose.

Tiny incisions are made both inside the nostrils and on the fleshy column between the nostrils (the columella). This enables the surgeon to lift the skin from a large portion of your nose, allowing access to the inside of the nasal cavity, so more significant changes can be made to the structural parts of the nose.

Revision Rhinoplasty: Revision, or secondary rhinoplasty, applies to any patient who has previously undergone a rhinoplasty procedure, but requires improvement to the appearance and / or function of the nose.

It is sometimes performed with septal surgery (the straightening or reshaping of the central cartilage and internal bony framework of the nose) and may use either the open or closed technique.

Non-Surgical Rhinoplasty: As the name suggests, this type of procedure is non-surgical and is performed using injectable filler (usually hyaluronic acid).

The filler is injected underneath your skin to smooth out the appearance of the nose and address any minor aesthetic issues.

This procedure is usually completed within 15 to 45 minutes with no downtime required.

Unlike traditional rhinoplasty, the results of non-surgical rhinoplasty are not permanent, generally only lasting between 6 and 18 months.
